# Passive Application‑Limited Analysis for TCP‑APP‑001 (run‑004)

*All artifacts referenced are read‑only.  No raw files have been modified.*

---

## 1. TARGET SEMANTIC PREDICATE – `HasTransportPendingData` (D)

**Definition (taken verbatim from `docs/diagnostic-state-machine.md`):**

> *HasTransportPendingData*: Application payload has already been submitted to the
> transport and remains unsent / queued at the transport send boundary.

The predicate concerns data **already handed off** to the TCP stack but **not yet
transmitted** on the wire.  It **does not** refer to data that is still owned by
the application or buffered in user space.

 > **Important** – An application‑level `sleep` (or any pause) does **not** imply
 > `D = false`.  Previously submitted data may still be waiting in the send
 > buffer after the application stops calling `send()`.
 >
 > **Note** – A sequence‑space outstanding estimate of zero (computed from the
 > observed cumulative ACKs) does **not** prove `D = false`; it only shows that
 > all *observed* transmitted bytes have been acknowledged.  Submitted‑but‑unsent
 > data may still reside in the transport's send‑queue.

Our analysis must therefore distinguish **what the passive observer can see** from
what would be required to *directly* observe `D`.

## 3. PASSIVE TCP TIMELINE (WIRE‑LEVEL OBSERVATIONS)

The passive capture `tcp_capture.pcap` (size = 17 360 bytes, 100 packets) was
validated (`tcpdump -nn -r …` → exit status 0, no truncation warning) and covers
the whole connection between:

```
192.168.88.20:59990 → 192.168.88.21:5202
```

### 3.1 Chronological packet classes (excerpt)

* **SYN** – packet #1 (18:37:27.315171) – flags `[S]`
* **SYN/ACK** – packet #2 (18:37:27.315472) – flags `[S.]`
* **Final handshake ACK** – packet #3 (18:37:27.315482) – flags `[.]`
* **Data bursts** – 48 packets with flags `[P.]` (sender → receiver).  Each shows a
  *sequence range* (e.g. `seq 1:7241`) and a *raw receive‑window* of **502** bytes
  (the sender’s view of the receiver’s advertised buffer).
* **Reverse ACKs** – 48 pure‑ACK packets (`Flags [.]` from the receiver) whose raw
  window values range from **419** to **641**.
* **FIN / termination** – two packets at the end of the capture (FIN from the
  sender, FIN/ACK from the receiver).

### 3.2 Direct (WIRE_DIRECT) fields

| Packet class | Visible fields | Evidence level |
|--------------|----------------|----------------|
| SYN / SYN‑ACK | src/dst IP, src/dst port, flags, sequence number, raw window, TCP options (MSS, SACK‑OK, TS, wscale) | **WIRE_DIRECT** |
| Data (`[P.]`) | src/dst, flags, `seq:start‑end`, ACK number, raw window (`win 502`) | **WIRE_DIRECT** |
| ACK (`[.]`) | src/dst, flags, `ack`, raw window (419 – 641) | **WIRE_DIRECT** |
| FIN | src/dst, flags, `seq`, `ack`, raw window | **WIRE_DIRECT** |

### 3.3 Derived (WIRE_DERIVED) quantities

* **Scaled advertised receive window** – the window‑scale option negotiated in the
  SYN/SYN‑ACK is `wscale 7`.  Scaled value = `raw win × 2⁷`.  Example: raw = 641 →
  scaled ≈ 82 048 bytes.
* **Sequence‑space outstanding estimate** – following the methodology of
  *TCP‑BASE‑001*, we derive an estimate of bytes that have been sent but not yet
  acknowledged by:
  
  1. Tracking the **high‑water mark** of TCP payload sequence numbers observed in
     data packets (including any segmentation due to GSO/TSO).
  2. Accounting for **SYN/FIN** sequence‑space consumption (the initial SYN and
     final FIN each consume one sequence number).
  3. Handling **retransmissions** that reuse previously‑sent sequence numbers – they
     do not increase the high‑water mark.
  4. Using the **cumulative ACK** values from pure ACK packets to determine the
     highest byte the receiver has confirmed.
  5. Considering **wrap‑around** if the 32‑bit sequence space were to overflow
     (not applicable in this run).
  
  The outstanding estimate at any packet is therefore:
  `outstanding = (max data seq + 1) – (max cumulative ack)`
  where `max data seq` is the largest payload byte number observed.
  This quantity is **WIRE_DERIVED** – it is an observer‑level estimate and **is not
  the kernel’s exact `bytes_in_flight`** counter.
* **Sender‑data idle intervals** – periods between the timestamp of the last
  `[P.]` packet of a burst and the timestamp of the first `[P.]` packet of the
  next burst.  All 20 intervals are on the order of **100 ms**, matching the
  controlled pause.

## 4. ALIGNING APPLICATION AND PASSIVE TIMELINES

Both the controlled timeline and the pcap timestamps are derived from the same
host clock (the sender VM).  The timeline uses **nanosecond‑resolution epoch
times**, while the pcap timestamps are printed in *human‑readable* form with
microsecond precision.  Because they originate from the same kernel clock, the
domains are **directly comparable** after converting the nanosecond values to
`seconds.microseconds` (division by 1 000 000 000).  No additional offset
calibration is required.

### 4.1 Mapping of events

For each repetition *i* we align:

* `post_send[i]` (application moment when the `send()` call returned) → the first
  data packet(s) belonging to the corresponding burst (the earliest `[P.]`
  packet whose sequence number is **≥** the byte offset implied by `post_send`).
* `app_sleep_start[i]` → the time immediately after the last data packet of the
  burst.  The capture shows a **quiet period** of ≈ 100 ms before the next data
  packet appears.
* `app_sleep_end[i]` → the moment just before the first data packet of the next
  burst; the receiver continues to send pure ACKs during the pause, confirming
  that all previously transmitted data has been acknowledged.

The alignment confirms that **after each data burst the sender stops emitting
new data for roughly the same duration as the application‑controlled pause**.
No new data appears until the next `pre_send` event, and the receiver’s ACKs have
caught up (outstanding ≈ 0).  This pattern holds for all 20 repetitions.

## 5. DERIVED QUANTITIES (WIRE‑LEVEL)

### 5.1 Advertised receive window
* **Raw window** – observed in ACK packets: values 419 – 641.
* **Scaled window** – applying the negotiated scale factor (7):
  * 419 × 128 ≈ 53 632 B
  * 641 × 128 ≈ 82 048 B

These numbers show that the receiver never advertised a zero window; the sender
was never blocked by the receiver’s buffer.

### 5.2 Sequence‑space outstanding estimate
* **High‑water mark** – final data packet carries `seq 1310721` (the last byte of
  the 1 310 720 B payload).
* **Cumulative ACK** – the last pure ACK reports `ack 1310722`.
* **Outstanding bytes** – computed as `high_seq - last_ack`.  During the pauses the
  value is **0 bytes** (or at most a few bytes due to packet granularity).

This estimate is **WIRE_DERIVED**; it is an observer’s view of the flight size and
should not be conflated with the kernel’s internal `bytes_in_flight` counter.

### 5.3 Sender‑data idle intervals
* Derived from the timestamps of the last `[P.]` packet of a burst and the first
  `[P.]` packet of the subsequent burst.
* All 20 intervals fall in the range **100 ms ± 0.6 ms**, matching the controlled
  100 ms pause.

## 6. CENTRAL QUESTION – “Is there a period with no transport‑pending data?”

For each of the 20 controlled pauses we observe the following passive pattern:

1. **Immediately after the last data packet of a burst** the receiver sends pure
   ACKs that advance the cumulative ACK to the end of the burst.
2. **No new data packets** are observed for ≈ 100 ms.
3. **When the next `pre_send` occurs**, a new burst of data packets begins.

Thus, *passive evidence shows a period where the sender’s outstanding data has
drained (outstanding ≈ 0) and no new data is emitted*, exactly matching the
application‑controlled pause.

> **Label** – *PASSIVE EVIDENCE CONSISTENT WITH NO TRANSPORT‑PENDING DATA*.

We deliberately do **not** label this as `D = false` because the predicate `D`
requires endpoint knowledge of the send‑buffer occupancy, which is not directly
observable.

## 8. APPLICATION‑LIMITED INFERENCE (COMPARISON TO DAPPER)

The **Dapper** passive‑analysis approach (as documented in the project’s TCP‑BASE‑001
analysis) treats a long inter‑packet gap – significantly larger than the measured
RTT – together with a non‑zero receive window as evidence that the sender is not
backlogged and is therefore *application limited*.

**Run‑004 provides exactly that pattern:**

* RTT on this LAN is on the order of **tens of microseconds** (the SYN‑ACK latency
  is ~300 µs).  The observed idle gaps are **≈ 100 ms**, i.e. three orders of
  magnitude larger.
* The receiver’s advertised window never drops to zero (scaled ≈ 50 KB – 80 KB).
* No loss, duplicate ACKs, or congestion signals are present.

Consequently, a Dapper‑style heuristic would *infer* a non‑backlogged or
application‑limited situation for each pause.

**However**, this inference is **heuristic** and does **not** provide the same
semantic guarantee as the `HasTransportPendingData` predicate.  `D = false`
requires *exact* knowledge that the transport’s send buffer is empty, which a
passive observer cannot obtain.

|-----------|----------------------|------------------------------|----------------|---------------------|-----------------------|

| **F** – `FlowControlAllowsNewData` | Remote-advertised receive credit permits at least one new data packet, given the relevant TCP sequence/window state. | Raw Window field values 419‑641 (WIRE_DIRECT) and scaled window ≈ 53 KB‑82 KB (WIRE_DERIVED); no zero‑window observed | **WIRE_DERIVED** (partial) | No obvious receiver‑window blocking is observed | Whether the receiver’s advertised credit is actually usable at each write moment (requires sender state) |
 | **C** – `CongestionControlAllowsNewData` | Congestion controller permits sending | No obvious loss/recovery or congestion‑limiting episode is visible in the passive trace (WIRE_INFERRED) | **WIRE_INFERRED** (potential) | No passive evidence of congestion or recovery limiting the sender | Exact cwnd or congestion‑control permission cannot be observed |

**Can a passive observer uniquely classify `APPLICATION_LIMITED`?**

* The observer can **infer** the condition (¬D ∧ F ∧ C) based on the heuristic
  evidence above, but because **D** itself is not directly observable, the
  classification remains *heuristic* rather than definitive.
* Therefore the observer should report **“PASSIVE EVIDENCE CONSISTENT WITH
  APPLICATION_LIMITED (heuristic)”** and note the lack of a concrete `D`
  observation.

## 10. OBSERVABILITY GAP

| What TCP exposes (passive) | What remains unobservable (without endpoint) | What exact signal would close the gap |
|---------------------------|---------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| TCP sequence/ACK behavior provides behavioral evidence, but submitted‑but‑unsent transport data is not visible. | Internal send‑queue / submitted‑but‑unsent occupancy (or explicit `HasTransportPendingData`). | Exposure of send‑queue occupancy or an explicit `HasTransportPendingData` predicate. |
| TCP exposes advertised‑window information directly and scaled window by derivation, but semantic usable credit requires sequence/window state. | Whether the advertised receive credit is actually usable for new data (requires sender state). | Explicit usable flow‑control credit, or a complete justified derivation from TCP state. |
| TCP does not directly expose cwnd, pacing state, or algorithm‑specific permission to send. | Endpoint congestion‑control permission or sufficient endpoint CC state (e.g., relevant cwnd/in‑flight/pacing state). | Endpoint exposure of congestion‑control permission (cwnd, in‑flight, pacing, etc.). |

In short, **passive TCP** can reveal *what happened on the wire* but cannot tell
the *internal state* of the sender required for a definitive `D` assessment.

## 11. SUMMARY OF FINDINGS (what the observer can report)

  * The controlled application performed **20 writes of 65 536 B** with a **≈ 100 ms** pause.
  * The passive capture is **valid** (no truncation, 100 % of packets captured).
  * The quantitative script analyzed **19 pauses with a following `pre_send`**; in **19/19** cases the **sequence‑space outstanding estimate** reached **zero** before the next `pre_send`. The zero‑to‑next‑`pre_send` durations (nanoseconds) ranged from **99 731 875 ns** (≈ 99.731875 ms) to **100 713 521 ns** (≈ 100.713521 ms), with a median of **100 569 413 ns** (≈ 100.569413 ms) and a mean of **100 478 735.58 ns** (≈ 100.47873558 ms). This demonstrates that the previously observed transmitted sequence space becomes cumulatively acknowledged during each pause; it does **not** mean `D = false`.
  * The receiver never advertised a zero window, and no congestion signals were observed.
  * **Passive evidence is consistent with an application‑limited (non‑backlogged) state**, but the predicate `HasTransportPendingData` (`D`) cannot be directly verified. The observer should therefore report the consistency result and label the classification as *heuristic*.
